## 1.1 Background and Problem Context

At Assumption College of Davao (ACD), learning-outcome evaluation forms part of academic review and quality-assurance activities. The process involves assessing Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILOs) and relating course-level evidence to broader program outcomes. This is consistent with Outcome-Based Education (OBE), which places intended learning outcomes at the center of curriculum and assessment. In the Philippine higher-education context, outcomes-based quality assurance is also reflected in Commission on Higher Education (CHED) Memorandum Order No. 46, Series of 2012, which establishes an outcomes-based and typology-based approach to quality assurance in Philippine higher education.

Outcome assessment requires more than collecting evaluation responses. Evidence must remain associated with the appropriate course, program, learning outcome, academic period, evaluation instrument, and stakeholder group so that it can be interpreted and reviewed over time. Goss (2022) describes student learning outcomes assessment in higher education as a complex process associated with competence and continuous improvement. Bennett et al. (2023) similarly found that assessment work requires sustained time and resources and has a long-term orientation that responds to external stakeholders.

The relationship between course-level and program-level outcomes is also important in this process. CILOs describe what students are expected to achieve within individual courses, while Program / Graduate Outcomes (GOs) represent broader outcomes associated with an academic program. Connecting these levels allows course-level evidence to be considered in relation to broader program expectations. Recent work on course-to-program outcome alignment likewise emphasizes systematic mappings and feedback loops as a means of supporting coherent outcome-based assessment and quality improvement (Derouich, 2025).

At ACD, the need that led to Project CLOIE emerged from consultations concerning learning-outcome monitoring and accreditation preparation. The project client described the intended system as a feedback and tracking system in which students evaluate the attainment of course outcomes and the resulting information is stored, analyzed, and reported at the course-outcome level and in relation to broader program outcomes. The project also established that the system should provide organized evidence for academic review rather than determine instructional or curricular changes automatically.

The evaluation context involves several stakeholder groups. The project includes students, graduating students, alumni, and industry partners as respondents, while faculty members and academic or administrative personnel manage or review evaluation activities according to their institutional responsibilities. Four institution-specific evaluation instruments form part of this context: the Post-Term CILO Evaluation Tool, Graduating Student Exit Survey, Alumni Evaluation Tool, and Industry Partner Internship Evaluation Tool. These instruments provide different sources of evidence at different points in the academic and post-graduation cycle.

Using multiple stakeholder perspectives can provide information that is not available from course-level assessment alone. Employer and industry participation, for example, can contribute to external quality-assurance processes by providing perspectives on graduate competencies and workplace expectations (Hou et al., 2022). Such information still requires interpretation within the institution's own academic and quality-assurance processes.

The resulting information therefore needs to retain its context. A response should remain associated with the evaluation activity, academic period, course or program, outcomes being evaluated, and stakeholder group from which it originated. Quantitative ratings also need to remain distinguishable from qualitative feedback. As assessment records accumulate across evaluation cycles, consistent organization becomes important for analysis, reporting, and subsequent academic review.

Digital analytics can help institutions organize and interpret educational data. Hernández-de-Menéndez et al. (2022) describe learning analytics as the measurement, analysis, and reporting of data about learners and their contexts. However, analytics remain dependent on the quality and structure of the underlying data and on appropriate human interpretation.

System CLOIE, or the **System for Comprehensive Learning Outcomes and Instructional Evaluation**, was developed as the technological response to this institutional need. Its purpose is to organize learning outcomes and evaluation activities, collect stakeholder responses, process quantitative and qualitative evidence, and present relevant analytics and reports to authorized users. Within continuous quality improvement, the system supports the collection and review of evidence while academic personnel remain responsible for interpreting findings and determining what action should follow. This distinction is consistent with the use of systematic data collection and analysis within academic quality-improvement cycles (Samuel & Farrer, 2025).

## 1.2 Problem Statement

Assumption College of Davao needs a more systematic way to manage learning-outcome and stakeholder evaluation evidence across courses, programs, academic periods, evaluation instruments, and respondent groups so that authorized academic personnel can review the evidence efficiently and use it for quality-assurance and continuous-improvement activities.

The project addresses the following manifestations of the institutional problem:

1. Learning outcomes and their relationships to academic programs need to be maintained in a consistent structure so that evaluation evidence can be associated with the appropriate course and program context.
2. Evaluation activities involve different instruments, academic contexts, and stakeholder groups, creating a need for controlled workflows for deploying evaluations and collecting responses.
3. Evaluation evidence needs to be consolidated into quantitative results, qualitative feedback, analytics, and reports that authorized academic personnel can review within their assigned institutional scope.
4. Historical evaluation information needs to retain its academic context so that evidence from different evaluation cycles can be reviewed and compared when appropriate.
5. Access to academic and evaluation information needs to be controlled according to user roles and institutional scope because evaluation responses and related academic information may require confidentiality.
6. The resulting evidence needs to be organized in a form that can support academic review, quality assurance, continuous improvement, and accreditation preparation without replacing the judgment and authority of academic personnel.

These problems establish the basis for the project objectives and are intended to be traceable to the corresponding system requirements and validation evidence.

## 1.3 Project Objectives

## General Objective

Project CLOIE aims to provide a centralized system for managing learning-outcome and stakeholder evaluation evidence to support academic review, quality assurance, continuous improvement, and accreditation preparation at Assumption College of Davao.

## Specific Objectives

* **OBJ-01.** Provide functionality for encoding, organizing, and managing Program / Graduate Outcomes (GOs), Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILOs), and their relationships within academic programs.

* **OBJ-02.** Provide digital workflows for conducting stakeholder-based evaluations through the required evaluation tools for students, graduating students, alumni, and industry partners.

* **OBJ-03.** Provide authorized academic personnel with dashboards and reports that consolidate relevant learning-outcome and stakeholder evaluation evidence for academic review, quality-assurance activities, continuous improvement, and accreditation preparation.

* **OBJ-04.** Protect evaluation and academic information through authenticated access, role and scope restrictions, controlled response handling, and confidentiality measures appropriate to the user's institutional responsibility.

The objective identifiers are intended to remain stable throughout the project documentation. Each objective will be linked in the Requirements Traceability Matrix to approved requirements, design components, implementation evidence, test or validation references, and the latest verified status. This avoids treating the objectives as statements that are automatically achieved simply because the software was developed.


## 1.4 Scope, Boundaries and Constraints

### 1.4.1 Project Scope

System CLOIE is a web-based academic evaluation and learning-outcome evidence system for Assumption College of Davao. Its scope covers the management of academic structures, learning outcomes, evaluation instruments and deployments, stakeholder responses, quantitative and qualitative evaluation evidence, analytics, and reports for authorized users.

The system manages Institutional Learning Outcomes (ILOs), Program / Graduate Outcomes (GOs), and Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILOs), including relationships between course and program outcomes. It also maintains academic context relevant to evaluation, such as academic periods, programs, courses, course assignments, faculty-course relationships, and course rosters where applicable.

The project includes the four institutional evaluation instruments represented in the CLOIE requirements: the Post-Term CILO Evaluation Tool, Graduating Student Exit Survey, Alumni Evaluation Tool, and Industry Partner Internship Evaluation Tool. The system supports evaluation deployment, response collection and handling, quantitative result processing, qualitative feedback, analytics, and reporting within the approved system scope.

CLOIE's analytics and reports are intended to provide organized evidence for academic review, quality assurance, Continuous Quality Improvement, program evaluation, and accreditation preparation. The system presents evidence for review; it does not independently determine curriculum changes, academic actions, or accreditation findings.

### 1.4.2 Users and Organizational Scope

System CLOIE supports different users according to their institutional responsibilities and authorized scope. The identified user groups are:

* **Secretary**, for administrative and evaluation-management responsibilities assigned to the role;
* **Dean**, for college-level oversight and access to information within the authorized college scope;
* **General Education Coordinator**, for General Education-related responsibilities and applicable outcome and course management;
* **Program Head**, for program-level outcome, course, evaluation, and evidence-management responsibilities;
* **Faculty**, for responsibilities associated with assigned courses and related learning-outcome and evaluation activities;
* **Student**, primarily as a respondent to assigned evaluations;
* **Alumni**, as respondents to applicable post-graduation evaluations; and
* **Industry Partner**, as respondents to applicable workplace or internship evaluations.

The system distinguishes between **role** and **scope**. A user's role determines the functions available to that user, while the user's scope determines the academic or organizational information that the user is authorized to access or manage. Depending on the role, this scope may be associated with a particular course, program, General Education responsibility, college-level responsibility, or respondent-specific evaluation activity.

The final responsibilities and access scope of each role are subject to the approved requirements and implementation evidence documented in Chapter 4 and the Requirements Traceability Matrix.

### 1.4.3 System Boundaries

System CLOIE is focused on academic evaluation and learning-outcome evidence management. It does not replace:

* the institution's Learning Management System;
* the Student Information System;
* an individual student grading system;
* instructional delivery platforms;
* the formal authority of academic decision-makers; or
* the institution's accreditation process itself.

CLOIE collects, manages, analyzes, and presents evaluation evidence. Academic and curricular decisions remain the responsibility of authorized institutional personnel.

The system does not treat evaluation analytics as automatic judgments about program quality. Quantitative results and qualitative feedback are evidence for review, while interpretation and subsequent institutional action remain outside the system's decision-making authority.

Peer-to-Peer Evaluation, Self-Evaluation, and Class Observation are proposed or pending features. They are not treated as completed baseline System CLOIE modules unless subsequent approved requirements, implementation evidence, testing, and validation establish otherwise.

### 1.4.4 Technical and Operational Constraints

The operation and evaluation of System CLOIE are subject to project and institutional constraints. These include dependence on internet connectivity and approved external services, availability of appropriate stakeholder and academic data, institutional requirements for user accounts and access, privacy and security restrictions, participant availability for validation, and any approval or operational requirements associated with institutional use.

These constraints may affect development, testing, validation, deployment, and eventual turnover. Their final status and impact should be documented through the project records and deployment evidence rather than assumed to be resolved.

## 1.5 Significance and Intended Beneficiaries

### 1.5.1 Assumption College of Davao

ACD may use organized evaluation evidence to support academic review, quality assurance, continuous improvement, and accreditation preparation. Centralizing relevant evidence can also reduce the need to consolidate information from disconnected evaluation records.

### 1.5.2 College Dean

The Dean may use authorized college-level views and reports to review learning-outcome and stakeholder evaluation evidence within the scope assigned to the role.

### 1.5.3 General Education Coordinator

The General Education Coordinator may use applicable functions for managing General Education-related course and outcome information and reviewing relevant evaluation evidence within the coordinator's authorized scope.

### 1.5.4 Program Heads

Program Heads may use learning-outcome information, evaluation evidence, analytics, and reports relevant to their assigned programs to support program-level review.

### 1.5.5 Faculty Members

Faculty members may use course-related learning-outcome and evaluation information to review evidence associated with their assigned courses and contribute to academic improvement activities.

### 1.5.6 Students

Students participate in applicable evaluation activities and provide feedback that becomes part of the evidence available for academic review and quality improvement.

### 1.5.7 Alumni

 Alumni provide post-graduation perspectives through the applicable evaluation process. Their responses contribute another source of evidence for program review.

### 1.5.8 Industry Partners

Industry partners provide external feedback through the applicable evaluation process. Their responses can provide information relevant to workplace and professional expectations.

### 1.5.9 ICTC and Future System Maintainers

ICTC personnel and future maintainers may benefit from having documented system requirements, architecture, configuration, security controls, and operational dependencies. These materials can support maintenance and institutional turnover when supported by the final project documentation.

### 1.5.10 Future Capstone Researchers or Institutions

The project may provide a reference for future work involving academic evaluation and learning-outcome evidence management. Any reuse or adaptation would depend on the requirements, policies, academic structures, and technical conditions of the adopting institution.

## 1.7 Definition of Terms

* **Course Intended Learning Outcome (CILO).** A learning outcome associated with a particular course that describes what students are expected to achieve through the course.
* **Institutional Learning Outcome (ILO).** An institution-level learning outcome representing broader learning expectations applicable within the institution's academic framework.
* **Program / Graduate Outcome (GO).** A broader outcome associated with an academic program and used in the project to represent expected competencies or achievements at the program or graduate level.
* **CILO Mapping Manifestation.** The relationship recorded in System CLOIE to describe how a CILO contributes to a broader program outcome through the approved manifestation categories of **Learning, Practice, or Opportunity**.
* **Course-Bound Evaluation.** An evaluation deployed within the context of a particular course, such as a Post-Term CILO Evaluation.
* **Central Evaluation.** An evaluation deployed at a broader institutional or program context rather than being tied to a single faculty-course evaluation workflow.
* **Controlled Deployment.** A configured evaluation activity that determines the intended respondents, evaluation context, availability, and response conditions for a particular evaluation instance.
* **Outcome Attainment.** Evidence representing the degree to which an intended learning outcome is demonstrated through the approved evaluation or assessment information available in System CLOIE.
* **Stakeholder Evaluation.** An evaluation in which an identified stakeholder group, such as a student, graduating student, alumnus, or industry partner, provides responses concerning an approved evaluation instrument.
* **Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI).** An ongoing institutional process in which evidence is reviewed and used to identify and implement appropriate improvements. In Project CLOIE, the system supports the evidence-collection and review aspects of this process but does not make academic decisions independently.
* **Project CLOIE.** The capstone project involving the analysis, design, development, testing, documentation, and validation of the proposed solution.
* **System CLOIE.** The software system developed by Project CLOIE to manage learning-outcome and academic evaluation evidence.
